# INTERNET-USAGE-DASHBOARD-ANALYSIS-FOR-ONLINE-APP-STARTUP

📊 Overview
This dashboard provides deep insights into how people of different ages use the internet, including:

Total screen time by age group
Breakdown of time spent on Social Media, Work/Study, and Entertainment
Primary devices used (Tablet, Mobile, Laptop)
Internet connection types (WiFi vs Mobile Data)

Built to help researchers, marketers, educators, and product teams understand generational differences in digital behavior.
🎯 Key Insights

Highest screen time: 26-35 age group with 4.1K hours
Youngest & oldest groups show very similar screen time patterns
26-35 age group spends the most time on work/study activities
Social media usage is consistently high across all age groups
Mobile phones and WiFi dominate as primary device and connection type

Key Analytical Insights:

The 26-35 age group clearly dominates with 4.1K hours, this is the peak “digital life” stage where career, social life, and personal entertainment overlap most intensely.
Surprisingly, 60+ ranks second highest (3.9K hours), beating every middle-aged group (36-60). This suggests strong retirement-era digital engagement (streaming services, video calls, online hobbies).
13-18, 36-45, and 46-60 all tie at 3.7K hours indicating remarkably stable screen-time habits outside the two peak cohorts.
Business implication: Marketers and app developers should prioritize the 26-35 and 60+ segments for maximum reach.

3. Total Hours Spent on Each Activity by Each Age Group

Extracted Data & Legend:

Teal = Total social media hours
Brown = Total work or study hours
White = Total entertainment hours

Notable exact value (tooltip):

13-18 work/study hours = 1,824 hours

Key Analytical Insights:

Social media (teal bars) is the most consistent activity across all age groups (~950–1,050 hours). This proves social media is a universal digital habit, not just a “young people” phenomenon.
Work/study (brown bars) shows the strongest life-stage variation:
Peaks dramatically in 26-35 (highest bar, ~2,000+ hours)  prime career-building years.
Also very high in 13-18 (explicitly 1,824 hours) reflecting intense student workloads.
Remains elevated in older groups (46-60 and 60+), possibly due to remote work, lifelong learning, or professional side activities.

Entertainment (white bars) stays flat (~900–1,000 hours) for every age  leisure screen time is age-agnostic.
Insight: Platforms should design age-specific features — heavy productivity tools for 26-35, study-focused modes for teens, and relaxed entertainment for all.

Key Analytical Insights:

Extremely balanced usage, all three devices differ by less than 1.4 percentage points.
Tablets edge out slightly, likely because they offer the best “lean-back” experience for browsing and media consumption.
No single device dominates, users are truly multi-device, choosing the right tool for the context (mobile for mobility, laptop for deep work, tablet for comfort).
Product implication: Every app or service must deliver seamless, identical experiences across tablet, mobile, and laptop to capture the full 23K+ hours of screen time.
